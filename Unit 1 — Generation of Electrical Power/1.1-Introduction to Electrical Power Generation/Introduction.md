## 1.1.1 Definition of Electrical Power Generation

Electrical Power Generation is the process of converting different forms of energy into electrical energy using suitable machines and equipment.

Examples of power generation methods include:

- Thermal Power Generation
- Hydroelectric Power Generation
- Nuclear Power Generation
- Gas Turbine Power Generation
- Solar Power Generation
- Wind Power Generation

---

## 1.1.2 Need for Electrical Power Generation

Electrical energy is essential for modern life, industrial development, and economic growth.

The major applications of electrical energy are:

1. **Domestic Applications:** Lighting, fans, refrigerators, and household appliances.
2. **Industrial Applications:** Operating motors, machines, and industrial equipment.
3. **Agricultural Applications:** Irrigation pumps and agricultural machinery.
4. **Transportation:** Electric trains, metro systems, and electric vehicles.
5. **Commercial Applications:** Offices, hospitals, schools, and shopping centres.
6. **Public Services:** Street lighting, water supply, and communication systems.

## 1.1.3 Basic Principle of Electricity Generation

Most conventional power plants use an electrical generator to convert mechanical energy into electrical energy.

This conversion is based on **Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction**.

### Faraday's Law

Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a conductor or coil changes, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced in it.

### Mathematical Expression

\[
e=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
\]

Where:

- \(e\) = Induced electromotive force (volts)
- \(N\) = Number of turns in the coil
- \(\Phi\) = Magnetic flux (webers)
- \(t\) = Time (seconds)

The negative sign represents Lenz's law.

### Energy Conversion in a Power Plant

**Energy Source → Prime Mover → Generator → Electrical Energy**

Example of a thermal power plant:

Chemical Energy → Thermal Energy → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy

In a hydroelectric power plant, flowing or falling water drives a turbine connected to an electrical generator.

## 1.1.4 Sources of Electrical Energy

The sources of electrical energy can be classified into conventional and non-conventional sources.

### A. Conventional Sources

Conventional sources are traditional energy sources used for electricity generation.

1. **Coal:** Used as fuel in thermal power plants.
2. **Natural Gas:** Used in gas turbine and combined-cycle power plants.
3. **Hydropower:** Uses the energy of flowing or falling water.
4. **Nuclear Fuel:** Releases heat through nuclear fission to generate electricity.

### B. Non-Conventional Sources

Non-conventional sources include alternative energy technologies, many of which use renewable resources.

1. **Solar Energy:** Converts sunlight into electricity using photovoltaic cells or solar thermal systems.
2. **Wind Energy:** Uses wind turbines to generate electricity.
3. **Biomass Energy:** Uses organic materials for energy production.
4. **Geothermal Energy:** Uses heat from the Earth's interior.
5. **Tidal Energy:** Uses the movement of ocean tides.
6. **Fuel Cells:** Convert chemical energy directly into electrical energy through electrochemical reactions.

**Note:** Renewable and non-renewable are more precise categories for describing whether an energy source is naturally replenished.

## 1.1.6 Main Components of a Power Generation System

### 1. Energy Source

Provides the primary energy required for electricity generation.

Examples: Coal, water, uranium, sunlight, and wind.

### 2. Prime Mover

Converts the available energy into mechanical energy when a rotating generator is used.

Examples: Steam turbines, water turbines, and gas turbines.

### 3. Electrical Generator

Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy through electromagnetic induction.

### 4. Transformer

Changes AC voltage levels to suitable values for transmission or distribution.

### 5. Switchgear and Protection

Protects electrical equipment and helps isolate electrical faults.

### 6. Transmission System

Carries bulk electrical power from generating stations to receiving substations.

### 7. Distribution System

Delivers electrical energy from distribution substations to consumers.

## 1.1.7 Advantages and Limitations of Electrical Energy

### Advantages

1. Can be transmitted over long distances using suitable transmission systems.
2. Can be distributed and controlled conveniently.
3. Can be converted into mechanical, thermal, and light energy.
4. Is widely used in domestic, industrial, and commercial applications.
5. Supports communication, automation, and modern technology.

### Limitations

1. Large-scale electricity storage can be expensive.
2. Generation and transmission require infrastructure and investment.
3. Some generation methods cause environmental pollution.
4. Transmission and distribution involve electrical losses.
5. Reliable supply requires proper planning, operation, and protection.

## 1.1.8 Important Technical Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Electrical Energy | Energy transferred or consumed through electrical processes |
| Electrical Power | Rate of transfer or conversion of electrical energy |
| Generator | A machine that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy |
| Prime Mover | A machine that drives a generator |
| Turbine | A machine that extracts energy from a moving fluid |
| Power Plant | A facility used to generate electrical power |
| Transmission | Transfer of bulk electrical power over a network |
| Distribution | Delivery of electrical power to consumers |
| EMF | Electromotive force, measured in volts |
| Efficiency | Ratio of useful output energy or power to input energy or power |

### Important Power Formulas

For a DC circuit:

\[
P=VI
\]

For a single-phase AC circuit:

\[
P=VI\cos\phi
\]

For a balanced three-phase AC circuit:

\[
P=\sqrt{3}V_L I_L\cos\phi
\]

Where:

- \(P\) = Active power (watts)
- \(V\) = Voltage (volts)
- \(I\) = Current (amperes)
- \(\cos\phi\) = Power factor
- \(V_L\) = Line voltage
- \(I_L\) = Line current

## 1.1.9 Short Answer Questions

**Q1. What is electrical power generation?**

Electrical power generation is the process of converting other forms of energy into electrical energy.

**Q2. State the operating principle of an electrical generator.**

An electrical generator operates on Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.

**Q3. What is a prime mover?**

A prime mover is a machine that converts energy from a source into mechanical energy to drive a generator.

**Q4. Give two examples of conventional energy sources.**

Coal and natural gas.

**Q5. Give two examples of renewable energy sources.**

Solar energy and wind energy.

**Q6. What is the function of a transformer in a power system?**

A transformer changes AC voltage levels according to transmission or distribution requirements.

**Q7. What is the function of a turbine in a power plant?**

A turbine converts energy from a moving fluid into mechanical rotational energy.

**Q8. What is the SI unit of electrical power?**

The watt (W).

## 1.1.12 Summary

Electrical power generation is a fundamental part of an electrical power system. Different energy sources are used to produce electricity according to their availability, cost, environmental impact, and technical requirements.

Generated electricity is transmitted through electrical networks and distributed to consumers for domestic, commercial, agricultural, and industrial applications.

Understanding the basic principles of power generation provides a foundation for studying different types of power plants, transmission systems, and distribution networks.
